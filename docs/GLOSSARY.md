# Glossary

Short definitions for **open-source intelligence (OSINT)** vocabulary, **conflict-monitoring** language, and terms you will see across Digital War Room. This is a reader’s guide, not legal or policy advice.

**In-product:** [Documentation hub → Glossary](https://digital-war-room.com/docs/documentation?doc=glossary). ## OSINT and intelligence disciplines

### OSINT (open-source intelligence)

Information derived from **publicly available** data: media, government releases, academic work, commercial datasets, ship and aircraft trackers, social posts, and other sources that do not require privileged access. OSINT emphasizes **verification**, **provenance**, and clear separation from classified or covert collection.

### All-source fusion (conceptual)

Combining multiple independent streams (news, markets, geography, cyber, diplomacy) into one assessment. Digital War Room automates a **subset** of this idea using open data only.

### GEOINT (geospatial intelligence)

Location-based insight: where activity occurs, how it clusters, and how it changes over time. In OSINT this often includes thermal hotspots, mapped events, and **AOI** (area of interest) context.

### SIGINT (in this product’s sense)

Classically, signals intelligence can include intercepts. Here, **SIGINT** refers to **open** military and maritime signals: e.g. aircraft **ADS-B** tracks, ship **AIS** positions, and public conflict reporting—not access to encrypted communications.

### SOCMINT (social media intelligence)

Patterns in open social and forum data: volume spikes, narrative themes, and notable posts. Useful for **early awareness**, not for attributing individuals without corroboration.

### FININT (financial intelligence)

Market and derivatives signals that may move with conflict risk: energy prices, prediction markets, and related indicators. Read as **stress and probability**, not trading advice.

### TECHINT (technical intelligence)

Infrastructure and connectivity: outages, censorship signals, exposure of internet-facing systems, and tech-export news. Helps describe **resilience and disruption**, not a full cyberattack timeline by itself.

### CYBER (threat intelligence, open sources)

Public **vulnerability** disclosures, threat reporting, and scanner context (e.g. background noise vs. targeted activity). Distinct from internal network defense or classified incident response.

### NEWS (open media)

Articles and headlines scored for volume and sentiment. Complements—not replaces—primary documents or field reporting.

### DIPLO (diplomatic / legal signals)

Sanctions lists, multilateral statements, and formal legal processes surfaced as structured signals.

### ENERGY and chokepoints

Commodity and storage stress plus **maritime chokepoint** traffic (e.g. constrained straits). Connects local conflict to **global market** and logistics risk.

### PROTEST and civil unrest

Public protest and riot indicators from event datasets and media, as a measure of **internal pressure** and instability.

### PROXIMITY (operational concept)

How close military-related activity is to **civilian** or protected infrastructure in open data—a **risk framing** tool, not a targeting solution.

### SATINTEL / imagery (where present)

Satellite imagery catalogues and product metadata used as **open** technical signals, not full imagery analysis tradecraft.

### HUMINT

Human intelligence from confidential sources. **Not** what this platform collects; included here because users confuse OSINT with human reporting.

### Verification and triangulation

**Verification** means checking claims against independent evidence. **Triangulation** means the same story holds when news, markets, geography, and another stream partially agree—raising confidence modestly, never certainty.

### Primary vs secondary sources

**Primary:** raw documents, original data releases, direct sensor feeds (with known limits). **Secondary:** summaries and analysis layers. OSINT workflows weight primaries higher when authentic and timely.

## Conflict background and geopolitical terms

### Escalation and de-escalation

**Escalation:** increase in intensity, scope, or risk of harm (military, economic, rhetorical). **De-escalation:** deliberate steps to reduce immediate risk. Dashboard scores are **indicators**, not predictions.

### Theater / area of operations (AOR)

The geographic and logical space where a conflict is monitored. The product may use a **conflict** label (e.g. a country or region) to scope queries and scoring.

### Gray-zone activity

Coercive actions **below** obvious armed conflict: cyber harassment, economic pressure, proxy harassment, information campaigns. Hard to score on a single chart; multiple streams help.

### Hybrid warfare

Blending military and non-military means (information, economic, cyber, proxies). Often overlaps with gray-zone framing.

### Proxy conflict

States or groups fight **through** intermediaries, complicating attribution and de-escalation paths.

### Ceasefire, humanitarian pause, truce

**Ceasefire:** broad halt to fighting (often fragile). **Humanitarian pause:** short, localized window for aid or evacuation. **Truce:** informal or partial; definitions vary by context. Treat labels from media with **source scrutiny**.

### Deterrence and brinkmanship

**Deterrence** aims to discourage an action by credible cost or risk. **Brinkmanship** deliberately approaches the edge of conflict to extract concessions—highly sensitive to misinterpretation in open data.

### Sanctions and export controls

Legal and economic restrictions on trade, finance, or technology. **OFAC** (U.S.) and EU lists are examples of **structured** open references; always use official registries for compliance decisions.

### Strategic chokepoint

A narrow sea passage or corridor where traffic concentration creates **economic** or **logistics** leverage. Distinct from “chokepoint” in networking or cybersecurity.

### Civilian infrastructure and dual-use

**Civilian infrastructure:** hospitals, power grids, water, schools. **Dual-use:** goods or tech with both peaceful and military relevance (e.g. certain chemicals, chips). Proximity and compliance features highlight **risk to civilians**, not legitimacy of strikes.

### Nuclear safeguards (high level)

**IAEA**-related monitoring and treaty language appear in public reporting. Treat open summaries as **starting points**; technical status belongs to official agency releases.

### Territorial dispute vs international armed conflict

Legal categories differ (border claims vs sustained hostilities). Open dashboards rarely resolve legal status; they surface **signals** consistent with user-selected context.

### Information environment

The mix of state media, independent outlets, social narratives, and bots. **Narrative** agents may compare sources; contradictions are expected under conflict stress.

### Humanitarian access and displacement

Aid reach and population movement often lag behind headlines. Indicators here are **imperfect proxies** for on-the-ground humanitarian status.

## How scores and labels relate to language

Composite scores and threat labels summarize **many weak signals**. They are designed for **situational awareness** and transparency into sources—not for command decisions, legal determinations, or life-safety choices. For methodology detail, see [Methodology](https://digital-war-room.com/docs/documentation?doc=methodology).
